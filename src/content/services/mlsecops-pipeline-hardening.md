## Who MLSecOps pipeline hardening is for

MLSecOpsHub hardens ML pipelines for platform teams that already move models at scale and want to stop trusting them by default. The typical starting point is a pipeline that pulls checkpoints from Hugging Face or an internal bucket, loads them in a CI job that also holds cloud credentials, and pushes the result to a registry that any engineer can overwrite. That pipeline works until a single artifact is malicious, mislabeled or silently replaced.

The engagement is implementation work. MLSecOpsHub writes the CI checks, the verification steps and the runbooks together with your platform engineer, in your repositories, with pull requests your team reviews and merges.

## What "hardened" means in practice

A hardened pipeline answers four questions for every model that reaches production:

1. **Where did it come from?** The source organization, repository and immutable revision are recorded and allowlisted. A model that arrives from an unlisted source or a floating tag does not enter the pipeline.
2. **Can loading it execute code?** The format is safetensors or another tensor-only format. Pickle-based files are rejected or quarantined for manual review. Loader code is reviewed and pinned to versions that enforce `weights_only` semantics, with CVE-2025-32434 as the reminder that the flag alone is not the control.
3. **Has it been altered?** The artifact is signed at build time and its provenance attestation names the training code commit, the data snapshot and the pipeline run. Both are verified at registry promotion and again at deployment admission.
4. **What can the pipeline reach if it goes wrong?** Each CI job runs with short-lived, narrowly scoped credentials. A compromised job can fail its own step; it cannot push to production or read unrelated secrets.

These controls map directly to the model supply-chain incidents in the [Agentic Attack Index](/data) and to the supply-chain items in the OWASP ML Security Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS.

## How the engagement runs

Work is organized in sprints of one to two weeks, remote-first, in EU and APAC time zones. The first sprint produces the pipeline inventory and the trust-boundary diagram, which becomes the shared reference for everything after. Subsequent sprints implement one control cluster each: formats and loaders, sources and scanning, signing and provenance, registry and runner privileges, deployment admission. Each sprint ends with merged pull requests and a short written status that your leadership can read without context.

The engagement is fixed-scope and quoted after a 30-minute call. The quote depends on the number of pipelines, the registry in use and how much of the CI is shared.

## What you receive at the end

You keep merged code, reusable workflow templates, an AI-BOM generator, and runbooks for the events that will happen: onboarding a new model source, rotating signing keys, and responding to a scanner alert. You also receive a control mapping to the Cyber Resilience Act, EU AI Act Article 15 and NIST AI RMF, which is what auditors ask for when they hear the word "supply chain."
